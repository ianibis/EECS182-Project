4. Task 4: decoding movement under stimulation

Three parts. Section 4.1 fixes a shared calibration-draw protocol so every method faces the same labelled trials. Section 4.2 builds an EEGNet decoder trained only on stimulation-off recordings and reports what it does when the stimulator is on. Section 4.3 runs the traditional alternative, PARRM cleaning plus a ridge refit on the calibration trials of each draw, on the same partitions.

4.1 Calibration draws: for each test session and k = 2, 5, 10 and 20, sample 10 sets of k stimulation-on trials; the remaining on trials are the query trials

Every test session holds 50 stimulation-on trials. Comparing a method that may learn from some ON labels with one that may not requires that both see exactly the same labelled trials, so this section fixes the partitions once and both later sections consume them.

For each of the 14 test sessions and each k in {2, 5, 10, 20} we draw 10 distinct random sets of k ON trials. The trials inside a set are the calibration trials, the only ON labels a method is allowed to use. The other 50 − k trials are the query trials, and those are what gets scored. Larger k therefore buys more labels and costs query trials.

Two consequences run through the rest of the section. Calibration labels are a shared resource: 4.2 spends none of them, which is why its rows carry k = 0 and appear once per session instead of once per draw, while 4.3 spends exactly k of them on a refit. And because the query set shrinks as k grows, scores at different k are not directly comparable: k = 20 is measured on 30 trials and k = 2 on 48.

4.2 EEGNet regression decoder (braindecode) on 1 kHz LFP, predicting kinematics per 50 ms window, trained on stimulation off trials

The decoder reads raw local field potential (LFP), the voltage on 35 electrodes implanted over motor cortex, sampled at 1 kHz and filtered 0.1–300 Hz. One input is one trial: 2,000 samples, 2 s, aligned to the go cue. The target is the index fingertip velocity from DeepLabCut tracking, in two components (vx, vy), binned into 40 windows of 50 ms, so the network emits one velocity pair per bin.

Training uses 14 stimulation-off sessions (560 trials) and validation uses 3 further stimulation-off sessions (150 trials). The 14 stimulation-on test sessions from 5 days are scored once and never trained on. Each test session is scored twice: on the stimulated recording itself, and on that day's stimulation-off recording, which we call the clean input. Scores are averaged per day rather than per session, because a day's sessions share one clean reference recording and so cannot be scored independently. The five days hold 1, 3, 3, 3 and 4 sessions.

The stimulated recordings carry a stimulation artifact, which is the difficulty this task exists to measure. Stimulation applies a 3.5 kHz carrier through the recording electrodes, amplitude modulated at 3 Hz or 20 Hz. The hardware removes part of the carrier and leaves the residue on the LFP. That residue repeats once per modulation cycle, every 333 ms at 3 Hz and every 50 ms at 20 Hz, so one 50 ms bin holds exactly one 20 Hz period or about 6.7 periods at 3 Hz. Three test days are 3 Hz and two are 20 Hz. Amplitudes throughout are in z units: each channel is centred on its own median and divided by its median absolute deviation, per channel and per recording, so 1 z is one robust standard deviation of that channel's own signal. Appendix A shows the artifact and gives its peak amplitudes.

4.2.1 Adapt braindecode's EEGNet from classification to regression with one kinematic output per 50 ms window

braindecode's EEGNet is a classifier: its temporal convolutions pad symmetrically, and its head pools the time axis away. Two changes turn it into a per-bin regressor. final_conv_length=1 with n_outputs=2 keeps one velocity pair per time step, and pooling 2 then 25 maps 2,000 samples to 40 bins of 50 ms. Every padded temporal convolution is rebuilt as an explicit left-only ConstantPad2d plus an unpadded convolution, so bin t depends only on samples at or before t. The result has 6,005 parameters.

The spatial block is a ParametrizedConv2dWithConstraint. In all ten saved models its 16 spatial filters sit at L2 norm exactly 1.000, so the max-norm constraint binds and only the direction of each spatial filter is learned, never its scale.

4.2.2 Write the training loop: early stopping on validation trials, and NaN kinematics masked out of the loss

Camera tracking drops out on some bins, so their targets are NaN and they must not contribute to the loss. Each trial carries a boolean mask mb that is 1 for a valid bin. The loss is the mean squared error over valid bins only:

L = (1 / M) · Σb mb · ‖v̂b − vb‖²,   M = Σb mb

where vb is the measured (vx, vy) pair of bin b and v̂b its prediction. Reported scores use per-axis R² averaged over the two axes:

R²axis = 1 − SSres / SStot = 1 − Σb(yb − ŷb)² / Σb(yb − ȳ)²,   R² = ½ (R²vx + R²vy)

y is one axis of the measured velocity, ŷ its prediction, ȳ its mean over the scored bins. R² = 1 is exact, R² = 0 equals predicting ȳ, and R² < 0 is worse than that constant. SStot is the recording's own variance, which makes R² unit-free and comparable across days.

One epoch is one pass over the 560 training trials in batches of 16, so 35 gradient steps. Muon updates the weight matrices and Adam the vectors, both on a cosine schedule that decays to 1% of its initial rate. After each epoch the model is scored on the 150 held-out trials; the best epoch's weights are kept, and training stops once 20 epochs pass without improving by more than 1e-4. The cap is 200 epochs. No test data enters this loop.

The architecture has three settings worth choosing: the temporal kernel length, the depth multiplier, and dropout. We trained eight configurations (the defaults, five single-knob changes, two paired changes), each once, and ranked them by validation R² at the best epoch.

4.3 Traditional pipeline: PARRM cleaning, then ridge refit on the k calibration trials of each draw

PARRM removes the artifact by exploiting its repetition. Average a stimulated recording at each phase of the modulation cycle and the artifact survives while the neural activity, which wanders between cycles, cancels; subtract that template from every sample and the residue is nominally artifact-free. It needs no labels, no kinematics and no calibration trials, which is what makes it attractive. The recording here is 0.1–300 Hz LFP on 35 electrodes, and the artifact repeats every 1/f seconds with f the modulation frequency.

The question this section answers is narrow. Task 3.2 already showed that cleaning a stimulated recording does not let a decoder trained on clean data transfer to it. The weaker hope is that cleaning adds something once the model is refitted on labelled stimulated trials anyway. So for each partition from 4.1 we fit ridge from scratch on the k calibration trials of that draw, with the penalty chosen on clean training data as in task 3.1, and score the 50 − k query trials. The only variable is whether the network sees the recorded ON features or the PARRM-cleaned ON features. The z-scoring statistics for the query trials come from the same draw, so cleaning cannot leak information across draws.

Two facts fall out. Refitting works: R² climbs from +0.004 at k = 2 to +0.046 at k = 20, a tenfold gain from 18 more labelled trials, and the same refitted model also improves on clean input (+0.001 to +0.031). Cleaning does not: PARRM lands within 0.0004 of the recorded input at every k, and always on the wrong side of it. The confidence intervals overlap completely.

Three reasons, all visible in this data, explain why cleaning adds nothing here. The repetition is only approximate, because artifact amplitude drifts within a session, so one template leaves a residual that is still large: channel 64 in session 250616-102853 reaches z ≈ 5300 under stimulation. Part of the brain signal is clock-locked to the stimulus too, so a phase-locked evoked response sits at the same phase as the artifact and template subtraction removes it along with the artifact. And a ridge model fitted on labelled ON trials already learns small weights on the artifact-dominated channels by itself, so cleaning overlaps with the job the labels were going to do.

These numbers are not directly comparable to 4.2's stimulated column. There the decoder is trained on clean sessions only and scored on all 50 ON trials per session; here the decoder is fitted from scratch on k ON trials and scored only on the 50 − k query trials, averaged over draws. The two protocols answer different questions, which is exactly why 4.1 fixes both up front.

Appendix B: Synthetic tone augmentation

This appendix is separate because the augmentation is a design choice, not part of the baseline evaluation. Everything in 4.2 and 4.3 is the plain pipeline; only the row marked "synthetic tones" in 4.2.3 used what follows. The baseline decoder never sees stimulation, so its stimulated-input score measures transfer to novel data rather than decoding skill. Adding periodic interference to the training trials narrows that gap without touching a single label: we superimpose a sine at the stimulator frequency on every training trial, which makes the training set look a little more like the test set.

Appendix D: Caveats and reproduction

These runs did not seed the weight initialisation: torch.manual_seed was called after the model was constructed, so the starting weights came from whatever the global RNG held at that moment, in a full notebook run the residue of the previous training call. The seed then controlled batch order and dropout only. That is why the sweep's seed-0 row (0.1463) and the seeds' seed-0 row (0.1459) disagree despite an identical best epoch of 48, and why rerunning one seed alone gives yet another model. The scores remain five honest samples of training variation, but that variation includes the starting weights, and a rerun of the corrected notebook will not reproduce these numbers.

  • The 4.2.2 sweep trains each configuration once, and its winning margin equals the standard deviation across runs.
  • Tone models are early-stopped on clean validation data, the distribution they were trained to distrust, so their stopping epoch is chosen on the wrong criterion. That can only understate them.
  • Day 250520 holds a single session; the other four hold three or four.
  • Validation R² (0.136) and the clean test score (0.021) are not comparable. The three validation sessions score 0.062, 0.197 and 0.150 individually, so the pooled 0.136 already carries the day-to-day spread that makes the five clean test days range from −0.106 to +0.132.
  • Five runs over five days cannot resolve a difference of ±0.03.
  • 4.3 and 4.2 use different protocols: 4.3 scores only query trials after a refit on k ON trials, 4.2 scores all 50 ON trials with a decoder that never saw ON labels.
